# Non-data pixels

Remove text without removing anything from the meaning. Write new text with the same restraint.

A line earns its place when it helps this reader understand, decide, or act here. A real number can be irrelevant. A short label can be empty. A longer explanation can be necessary. Do not optimize for word count.

## Start with the actual task

Honor the requested mode: review, rewrite, or implement. A review-only request stays read-only. A request to fix copy authorizes the relevant edits; do not stop at recommendations or ask for routine approval. Stay within the requested surface. This skill does not authorize publishing, sending, unrelated redesign, or changing product behavior.

Read the copy in context, including headings, values, buttons, nearby messages, and relevant states. Use the actual source or screen when available. For a pasted passage, work with what is given; investigate only when a missing fact affects the edit.

Identify the reader, the task, and whether this is a first visit or daily work. Find the product's established names and verbs. Reuse its accepted components and copy where they fit. Do not invent a new voice during a local edit.

For interface work, read [Interface examples](references/interface-examples.md) when a deletion, state change, or interaction needs judgment. For articles, marketing, specifications, or client updates, read [Writing by context](references/writing-by-context.md). These references include exceptions that prevent over-cutting.

## Decide whether each line belongs

Before polishing a sentence, ask what the reader loses if it disappears. Choose the smallest useful change:

- **Delete:** The surrounding interface already carries the meaning, or the line has no value for this task. Do not replace it with different filler.
- **Shorten:** The fact matters, but the sentence adds effort. Preserve the subject, action, scope, and consequence that matter.
- **Move:** The information matters at another moment. Show a submission status with the submitted item. Put optional detail in accessible help or an expanded view.
- **Keep:** The line supplies a needed distinction, instruction, state, value, or consequence.
- **Fix the cause:** The text compensates for a confusing control or false state. If behavior changes are outside scope, identify the specific problem instead of disguising it with copy.

Keep this reasoning internal unless the user requests an audit or the choice needs explanation. Do not produce a classification table for every ordinary edit.

## Write in this voice

- Use common words, concrete nouns, and the product's own terms. Prefer “Vendors” to “Who carries it?”
- Use direct verbs for instructions. In prose, make the actor clear when that helps. Labels and states can be noun phrases: “Name,” “Pending review,” and “Shared microphone” are not automatically passive-voice defects.
- State what something does. Avoid decorative contrasts such as “not just,” “rather than,” or “this isn't X, it's Y.” Keep a negative fact when it explains a real limit or consequence.
- Remove metaphors, personification, slogans, grand claims, invented jargon, and phrases that merely announce the explanation to follow.
- Do not give every heading, option, or field a second line. Do not teach a familiar workflow on every visit.
- Make questions warm and direct. “What happens after this?” is better than “Please finish the sentence.” Avoid scolding and baby talk.
- Use no em dashes or en dashes in authored text. Preserve supplied source quotations when the task requires exact reproduction.
- Do not invent facts, capabilities, personal experience, delivery promises, or completion claims to make the writing sound natural.

## Preserve the information people need

Keep the next step in an empty state. Keep unfamiliar concepts understandable on first use. Preserve units, dates, prices, scope, source distinctions, relevant uncertainty, error recovery, and material consequences when the reader needs them.

Do not remove accessible names with visible labels. Do not hide essential instructions in hover-only help. Check touch and keyboard access when moving help. A label used on two separate screens may be needed on both; duplication is about the reader's context, not repeated strings in source code.

Remove internal test instructions and build notes from ordinary product copy. Keep any real user-facing limit clear through accurate state and behavior. Do not make a simulated action look successful in a live system. Put developer details in the test surface or work report when appropriate.

Do not apply a universal ban on dates, headings, icons, “Coming soon,” format hints, or helper text. Their value depends on the task. Do not erase uncertainty, evidence, or honest qualifications under the label of cleanup.

## Match the form

- **Routine UI:** Established labels, useful values, direct actions, and current state.
- **Empty state or first use:** Enough explanation to start. Provide deeper help where needed.
- **Marketing:** Concrete benefit and relevant features. A short heading plus one useful sentence can work.
- **Article:** Introduce the subject before discussing it. Use connected, natural prose and necessary evidence. Do not turn it into clipped UI labels.
- **Client update:** Write as the user, first-person singular. Use past tense for completed work and direct language for new capabilities. Distinguish designed, built, and published.
- **Current specification:** Describe the current behavior and necessary unresolved decisions. Remove obsolete revision history.

Keep chat replies to one to three short sentences, with the main point in bold. Deliverable files can be detailed. Do not add a long explanation of how concise the result is.

## Finish the work

Read the result as the intended reader. Can they identify the object, understand the state, and take the next action? Does a needed fact disappear? Did the edit introduce a promise or change meaning?

For implemented UI edits, check relevant dynamic values and empty, loading, success, and error states. Inspect the changed layout when available: deleted text should not leave blank space or broken alignment. Use checks proportional to the change. Report a material verification limit plainly.

Return the finished text or the concrete review findings requested. For an audit, give the location, exact text, proposed change, and a short reason. Do not force the user through a large approval table before making already-authorized edits.

Treat explicit user approval as approval of the specific result. An assistant saying “Done,” silence, or a topic change does not establish preference. When adapting this skill later, distinguish user-requested wording, verified approval, and your own suggestion.
