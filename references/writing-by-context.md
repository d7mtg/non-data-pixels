# Writing by context

Adapt the writing to the reader and purpose. Preserve the information each form needs.

## Articles and explanations

Introduce the subject before the argument. Explain what the topic is, the relevant context, and why it matters to the reader. Develop the evidence or question in connected prose.

Use natural transitions and direct openings. Remove decoration that adds no useful context. Keep sources, uncertainty, and distinctions needed for accuracy. Ground anecdotes in supplied facts; do not invent personal experience.

For a routine text edit, retain supplied facts. Research when the task or an unresolved factual issue requires it.

## Marketing and onboarding

Describe a concrete benefit and the features that make it possible. Use the product's terms and actual capabilities. A short heading and a useful sentence may be enough.

When multiple steps form one flow, let each step add information rather than repeating the introduction. Keep action conditions visible where people need them. Do not invent urgency, evidence, customers, or outcomes.

## Client updates and handovers

Match the sender and purpose. First-person singular and past tense can suit an individual reporting completed work. Use present tense for current behavior and direct language for confirmed new capabilities.

Group work by a screen or flow the reader recognizes. Distinguish design, implementation, and release; do not imply a working feature exists merely because it was designed. Omit internal process and private details unless they are needed and authorized. Keep material delivery limits clear.

Ask questions in a warm, direct way. For example, “What happens after this?” can invite a useful response without scolding.

If a dedicated handover skill is available and relevant, use it for the requested document format and workflow. This guidance does not authorize sending a document.

## Specifications, manuals, and working documents

Describe the current system for a fresh reader. Preserve necessary unresolved decisions and distinguish intended behavior from implemented behavior. Remove abandoned approaches unless version history is the purpose.

Use direct actions in instructions. Include prerequisites, results, and recovery steps when needed to complete the task. Explain unfamiliar terms when the reader needs them.

## Replies in chat

Lead with the useful result. Match length and detail to the question and reader. Use paragraphs, lists, and emphasis where they help, and state material limits plainly.

Cut commentary that merely announces cleanup or repeats the answer. Include a next step when it helps the reader act.
